Do Solar Panels Need Maintenance?

For the most part, no. Solar panels have no moving parts and are built to sit outdoors for decades in all kinds of weather. They are one of the lowest- maintenance improvements you can make to a home — but there are a few small things worth knowing.

Cleaning

In most places, rain does the cleaning for you. Panels only really need a wash if they get visibly dirty — from heavy dust, pollen, or bird droppings — and even then a rinse with a hose is usually enough. In dry, dusty regions you might clean them a few times a year; in rainy climates, rarely if ever. Dirty panels lose only a small amount of output, so there is no need to obsess over it.

Keep an eye on production

Most systems come with monitoring (often an app) that shows how much electricity you are generating. Glancing at it now and then is the single most useful thing you can do: a sudden drop is your cue that something — shading, a dirty panel, or an inverter fault — needs attention.

What can wear out

  • The inverter is the part most likely to need replacing. A central "string" inverter typically lasts 10 to 15 years, so one replacement over the system's life is common. Microinverters generally last longer.
  • The panels themselves rarely fail. They are usually warrantied for 25 to 30 years and simply become slightly less efficient over time — on the order of 0.5% per year, an assumption the SunWise calculator already builds in.
  • Shading and debris — trimming overhanging branches and clearing leaves or heavy snow (only where it is safe to do so) keeps output up.

What it costs over time

Realistically, ongoing costs are small: the occasional clean, and typically one inverter replacement at some point. Because the panels keep producing with so little intervention, maintenance rarely changes the overall financial picture very much.
