Is Solar Worth It in 2026?

For a great many U.S. homeowners, solar is worth it — but it genuinely depends on your situation. The honest answer is that solar pays off well when a few conditions line up, and much more slowly when they do not. Here is how to tell which side of the line your home falls on.

Solar tends to be well worth it when…

  • Your electricity rates are high. The more you pay per kWh, the more each unit of solar power saves you — this is the single biggest factor.
  • You get good sunlight. Sunnier locations produce more energy from the same system.
  • You own your home and plan to stay. Solar pays back over years, so the longer you stay, the more you benefit.
  • Your roof suits it. An unshaded roof with decent orientation gets the most out of a system.

It is less compelling when…

  • Your electricity is cheap. Low rates mean smaller savings and a longer payback.
  • Your roof is heavily shaded or faces in a suboptimal direction.
  • You may move soon. If you sell before payback, the financial case rests on how much value solar adds to the sale.

The economics in plain terms

In much of the U.S., a residential system breaks even in roughly 6 to 10 years. After that, the panels keep producing low-cost electricity for many more years — most systems are warrantied for 25 to 30 years and often last longer. Everything after payback is effectively savings.

One change to factor into the math: the 30% federal residential solar tax credit ended on December 31, 2025, so it no longer shortens the payback for homeowners buying a system in 2026. Many state and local incentives do remain, and they vary widely, so check what currently applies where you live.

Beyond the money

There are non-financial reasons homeowners choose solar too: protection against rising electricity prices, a degree of energy independence, and lower carbon emissions. These do not show up in a payback figure but matter to many people.
